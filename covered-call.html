Every Monday, if flat, buy 100 AAPL shares at the print and write one Friday-expiring call. The strike is chosen by solving for the listed strike closest to a target delta — the target itself shifts with the current implied-vol regime (further OTM when vol is rich, closer to the money when it's calm) and pulls in further after a losing week, so a stock drop gets more room to recover before the position risks getting capped again. Friday: OTM expires (keep the shares, keep the premium, write the next week's call without re-buying); ITM is assigned (shares sold at the strike, flat, restart Monday).

NAV, initial & maintenance margin

Reg T account. NAV = cash + stock market value (the short call is closed out by the time each week's mark is taken, so it never carries a separate negative MV into the weekly snapshot). Initial margin is 50% of stock LMV; maintenance is 25%. A covered short call adds $0 to either, since the position isn't naked.

Write-up

Why this stock

AAPL was chosen for liquid weeklies and tight, well-quoted markets, which makes the midpoint fill assumption easier to defend than it would be on a thin name.

How the strike is chosen

The strike is picked by targeting a Black-Scholes delta rather than a fixed dollar distance from spot. The target itself is not constant: it slides between a calm-market value and a high-vol value based on where current implied vol sits in its own recent history, and it's pulled down further — capped at a floor so it can't go arbitrarily low — after a week where the stock lost money. A lower target delta means a strike further out of the money, i.e. more room before the position risks being capped again.

Wait through expiry

No rolls, no buy-to-close. A written call either expires worthless (shares and premium both kept, next week's call written without re-buying) or is assigned (shares delivered at the strike, position flat, restart the following Monday).

Fill assumption

Every fill uses mid = (BID + ASK) / 2 at the order timestamp. The scatter above tests this directly against real trade prints on the strikes actually used in the backtest.

Why Reg T, not portfolio margin

Reg T is computable without a broker's PM/SPAN engine and isn't broker-dependent. A book that survives Reg T's margin requirements survives portfolio margin too — the reverse isn't guaranteed.

Known limitations

The option leg's daily mark between Monday's write and Friday's resolution is not tracked intraweek — NAV reflects the position's value at each week's close, not a continuous daily mark-to-market of the open short call. The vol-regime signal is also noisier in the earliest weeks of the backtest, before enough implied-vol history has accumulated to make the calm/high-vol comparison statistically meaningful.
