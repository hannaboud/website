What this shows

Two results don't fit that pattern cleanly, and are worth naming rather than hiding. XOM rallied +18.5% yet the strategy still slightly outperformed (+23.3%) — most likely because assignment resets the position at a fresh cost basis the following Monday, and if the stock dipped briefly right after an assignment before continuing to climb, re-entering lower can occasionally beat a single static buy-and-hold even in an uptrend. IBM is the sharper outlier: the stock was down only -4.7%, yet the strategy lost -14.9%, a -10.2-point gap in the wrong direction. IBM was assigned 7 of 13 weeks — far more than any other name — meaning it wasn't a smooth decline but a choppy one, repeatedly rallying just enough to trigger assignment before falling again. That pattern (frequent short-lived rallies inside a longer downtrend) is close to the worst case for this strategy: it caps the rallies on the way up without ever getting the benefit of riding a sustained recovery.
