What this shows

The more a stock rallied over the window, the more the covered call strategy tended to lag simple buy-and-hold — visible clearly at the top of the table (PYPL -12.95%, JNJ -11.70%) and bottom (INTC +0.30%, NKE +3.03%). In stocks that fell or stayed flat, the strategy consistently matched or beat plain stock ownership: the premium collected cushioned the decline, and assignment was rare, so most weeks kept full exposure to any recovery.

Two results don't fit that pattern cleanly, worth naming rather than hiding. XOM rallied +18.5% yet the strategy still slightly outperformed (+23.3%) — most likely because assignment resets the position at a fresh cost basis the following Monday, and a brief dip right after assignment before the stock continued climbing let the strategy re-enter lower than a single static buy-and-hold. IBM is the sharper outlier: down only -4.7%, yet the strategy lost -14.9% — a -10.2-point gap in the wrong direction. IBM was assigned 7 of 13 weeks, far more than any other name, meaning it wasn't a smooth decline but a choppy one: repeated short-lived rallies inside a longer downtrend, each one capping gains right before the stock fell again. That pattern is close to the worst case for this strategy — it caps every rally on the way up without ever getting the benefit of riding a sustained recovery.
